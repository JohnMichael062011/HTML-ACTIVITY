Reactive oxygen species (ROS) is produced by aberrant stimuli such as ultraviolet (UV) radiation, which can damage DNA and cellular proteins. Previous studies have demonstrated that ROS activates inflammatory signaling pathways (Chen et al., 2006; Tormos, Talens‐Visconti, Nebreda, & Sastre, 2013). We also found that N‐acetyl cysteine (NAC, a ROS scavenger) drastically inhibited sUV‐induced matrix metalloprotein‐1 (MMP‐1) expression (data not shown) and siRNA of p47phox, a subunit of NADPH oxidase (NOX); as a result, reduction of EGFR transactivation in NADPH oxidase was observed (Chen et al., 2006). ROS also mediates UV‐induced skin inflammation via activation of inflammatory signaling pathways (Choi et al., 2016; Cooper & Bowden, 2007). Here, we demonstrated the potential of rose petal extract (RPE) on sUV‐induced skin inflammation via suppression of MAPK activation. Our results showed that RPE exerts strong antioxidant effects through cytokine suppression in epidermal cell lines exposed to sUV.
