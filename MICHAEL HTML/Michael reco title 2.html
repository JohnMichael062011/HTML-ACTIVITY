RECOMMENDATION

To promote sustainable agricultural productivity, it is recommended that land managers and policymakers transition from large-scale monocultures to complex, patchy landscapes by integrating a diverse mix of non-crop habitats. This research demonstrates that maintaining a high proportion of herbaceous margins and wooded patches can enhance natural enemy populations in up to 74% of cases, providing a biological buffer against pest outbreaks. Stakeholders should prioritize landscape diversification—specifically increasing field edges and fallow areas—as these features facilitate the spillover of predators and parasitoids into croplands, effectively suppressing pest pressure in nearly half of the studied scenarios. However, because higher predator density does not always correlate directly with reduced crop injury in current literature, it is further recommended that future monitoring programs explicitly track crop yield and damage metrics alongside biodiversity counts. Adopting this holistic landscape approach ensures the conservation of essential ecosystem services while reducing the long-term dependency on chemical pesticides.
