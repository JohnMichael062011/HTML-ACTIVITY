OBJECTIVES

The primary objective of this research is to test the hypothesis that natural pest control is significantly enhanced in complex, patchy landscapes with a high proportion of non-crop habitats compared to simplified, large-scale landscapes. The study aims to synthesize existing research to determine if higher landscape complexity leads to increased natural enemy populations and a measurable decrease in pest pressure. Additionally, the review seeks to evaluate the relative importance of different landscape factors—including herbaceous habitats, wooded habitats, and general landscape patchiness—in supporting biodiversity. Ultimately, the research intends to provide a scientific basis for the conservation of diversified landscapes as a means to sustain essential ecosystem functions like natural pest suppression.
