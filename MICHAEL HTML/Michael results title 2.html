RESULTS

The review confirms that landscape complexity plays a vital role in pest management, with 74% of the studies showing higher natural enemy populations and 45% reporting lower pest pressure in complex versus simple landscapes. While enhanced predator activity was clearly documented, the study found that lower crop injury—the ultimate goal of pest control—has rarely been recorded in the literature. Regarding specific habitats, herbaceous areas (like fallows and margins) were most effective, contributing to higher natural enemy activity in 80% of cases, followed closely by wooded habitats (71%) and landscape patchiness (70%). These similar percentages suggest that all forms of non-crop habitat are equally essential, leading to the conclusion that diversified landscapes are the most effective strategy for simultaneously conserving biodiversity and maintaining sustainable natural pest control.
