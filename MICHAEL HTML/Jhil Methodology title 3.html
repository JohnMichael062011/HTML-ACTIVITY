METHODOLOGY

The researchers developed a new approach by using banana peel starch as the primary biopolymer and adding corn starch as a co-biopolymer. To assess physical durability, the tensile strength is calculated by dividing the weight load by the cross-sectional area of the biofilm. The water absorption test involves cutting samples into 1\text{ cm} \times 2\text{ cm} pieces, recording their initial weight, and submerging them in 60 mL of water for 24 hours. After wiping the samples dry, the final weight is recorded to calculate the percentage of water uptake using a standardized mass-change formula. For chemical and thermal characterization, Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR) is utilized, where samples are placed on a germanium plate and scanned 256 times between 4000\text{ cm}^{-1} and 400\text{ cm}^{-1} to identify chemical bonds. Additionally, Differential Scanning Calorimetric (DSC) analysis is conducted using a Mettler Toledo 822e system, heating the samples from -10\text{ °C} to 250\text{ °C} at a rate of 10\text{ °C/min} under a nitrogen atmosphere to observe thermal transitions.
