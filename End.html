Once again, the people stood together. Louder than before, they filled the air with sound and faith, reminding the heavens that light was meant to be shared, not consumed. Overwhelmed, Minokawa loosened its grip and the sun burst free, flooding the land with warmth and life. Defeated but not destroyed, Minokawa retreated to the far edges of the sky, where it remains to this day—watching, waiting, and longing for the light. And so, whenever the sun or moon is briefly hidden, people remember the tale of Minokawa, a reminder that even in the face of overwhelming darkness, unity and courage can call the light back home.
